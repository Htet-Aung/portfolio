## Why I built oxyDoku

Outside of code, I play rapid chess (1750 on Chess.com) and spend a lot of time on hard Sudoku. In both games, looking up the answer is only useful if it teaches you how to recognize the idea yourself next time.

Once I moved past basic singles and subsets into Wings, Fish, Almost Locked Sets, and Alternating Inference Chains, I kept running into the same three walls:

1. **No easy way to scan a board for all patterns at once.** Most solvers only reveal the single next step in a fixed priority order, hiding everything else sitting in the same position.
2. **No structured online course for advanced logic.** Guides online mostly dump dozens of named techniques into flat lists without connecting the underlying rules or walking through why a deduction holds.
3. **No targeted way to practice spotting specific patterns.** Reading an explanation makes it easy to nod along, yet I had no way to drill one technique on demand, test myself on a blind mix, or verify my own eliminations directly on the grid.

I built **oxyDoku** on top of the open-source [AImenes/sudokUI](https://github.com/aimenes/sudokUI) codebase (for [sudokui.org](https://sudokui.org)) to fix those gaps for my own study. It keeps `sudokUI`'s local-first playing workspace and extends it into a 76-technique position scanner, a concept-driven learning hub with case-by-case proofs, an interactive practice lab, a chain trainer, and a live board sync extension.

## System architecture

To keep full-board pattern scans responsive in the browser while running identically on desktop, mobile, and tests, oxyDoku separates the board model and solver engine from React state and the learning UI. The core engine is pure TypeScript with zero DOM dependencies, shared across the web app, the Chrome Extension bridge, and the Capacitor Android wrapper.

## Scanning every pattern and comparing proofs

When I get stuck on a tough board, seeing only the cheapest legal step rarely tells me what I missed. Out of an 80-technique catalog (79 human techniques plus a brute-force fallback), oxyDoku scans **76 implemented human solving techniques** (`IMPLEMENTED_TECHS`) without mutating the board: **55 exhaustive finders** (singles, intersections, subsets, basic and finned fish, wings, uniqueness, and coloring) alongside **21 bounded graph and template finders** (`X-Chain`, `XY-Chain`, `AIC`, `Grouped Nice Loop`, `ALS-XZ`, `ALS-XY-Wing`, `ALS-XY-Chain`, `Death Blossom`, `Aligned Pair Exclusion`, `Forcing` chains, and `Exocet`).

Enumerating every pattern simultaneously created two practical problems in `src/engine/scanner.ts`:

1. **Keeping deep searches responsive without hiding limits.** Branching chains and Almost Locked Set finders can blow up on open grids. Each bounded finder runs under explicit limits in `BOUNDED_TECH_BOUNDS` (`maxLength`, `nodeBudget`, `maxAls`, `maxTemplates`, or `maxBranchSize`). When a search hits a limit, `truncated` and `truncationReason` (`search-budget`, `instance-cap`, or `template-overflow`) trigger a `"Search budget reached"` notice in the scanner panel instead of quietly dropping results.
2. **Deduplicating symmetric paths and comparing overlapping proofs.** Different search orders often rediscover the same chain backward, rotate the same continuous loop to another starting cell, or flag a reducible superset (like a Swordfish built from an X-Wing plus an extra line). `patternInstanceKey` canonicalizes reversed chains, rotated loops, swapped parity colors, and multi-unit subsets, while `irreducibleOnly` suppresses redundant supersets. At the same time, different techniques that eliminate the same candidate are kept separate and indexed in `byConclusion` under atomic keys (`E:cell:digit` and `P:cell:digit`), so the **By Outcome** view lines up competing proofs (such as an `ALS-XZ`, an `ALS-XY-Wing`, and an `ALS-XY-Chain`) side by side.

## Structured learning: concepts, guides, and step-by-step proofs

A solver that lists pattern names still leaves a beginner staring at 76 separate tricks. To turn oxyDoku into something closer to a structured course, I built four connected learning layers across `src/content/` and `src/ui/`:

- **Nine reasoning concepts instead of a flat list:** `TECHNIQUE_TAXONOMY` in `src/content/concepts.ts` organizes the catalog under **9 core reasoning concepts** (_Locked Sets & Subsets_, _Fish Logic & Transposition_, _Single-Digit Chains & Turbot Patterns_, _Wing & Bent Subset Logic_, _Coloring & Alternating Inference Chains_, _Almost Locked Sets (ALS)_, _Uniqueness Deadly Patterns_, _Contradiction & Forcing Chains_, and _Overlays & Exotic Patterns_). Each entry links aliases and relationships (`specialCaseOf`, `generalizes`, `related`), showing how a Skyscraper and a 2-String Kite are both 4-node Turbot/X-Chains, or how XY-Wings and XYZ-Wings act as small `ALS-XZ` interactions.
- **Personal mastery and Discovery Mode:** In `My Techniques` (`src/content/mastery.ts`, `MyTechniquesDialog.tsx`), I can pick a starter preset or tag each technique as `Known`, `Learning`, or `Unfamiliar`. Nothing is ever marked known automatically just because I viewed a hint. Everyday scans stay scoped to patterns I know or am studying, and toggling **Discovery Mode** flags unfamiliar patterns sitting on a live board when I want to see what else applies.
- **Guides with pitfalls and false friends:** `sudokUI`'s Learn hub already includes an Intuition guide (explaining core rules formally and in plain, notation-free language), a linked glossary, worked examples, and a frequency-ordered learning path. In `src/content/techniqueGuides.ts`, oxyDoku pairs those entries with structural roles, affected candidates, **common pitfalls & false friends** (lookalike shapes that break the rule, such as a finned fish whose fins cross boxes or an XY-Wing whose pincers see each other), distinctions from related patterns, inline familiarity toggles, and buttons to jump straight to practicing or scanning the live board.
- **Case-by-case step proofs and visual walkthroughs:** Rather than printing one dense line of cell notation, `src/content/stepExplainer.ts` uses 14 specialized builders to break any scanned pattern or hint into a **Core Principle**, **Case-by-Case Proof** branches (for example, tracing both candidate values of a Wing pivot), step-by-step causal logic, and per-candidate elimination reasons. `FormattedExplanation.tsx` highlights coordinates, rows, columns, boxes, and digits with theme-aware color tokens, while `Walk through it` steps through the board drawing one idea at a time.

## Practicing specific patterns and building chains

To move from reading explanations to recognizing patterns under real board clutter, I built an interactive Practice Lab and paired it with `sudokUI`'s SVG Chain Trainer:

- **Focused and mixed-technique drills:** Across **61 practisable techniques**, Focused Practice drops me into a generated puzzle position where that exact pattern is the next logical move. When I want realistic pattern-recognition testing without knowing the answer ahead of time, **Mixed-Technique Practice** pulls blind drills from my `Known` and `Learning` techniques so I have to figure out which pattern is actually on the board.
- **Direct board verification and 4-stage progressive hints:** In `PracticePanel.tsx`, I can strike out candidate notes or place digits directly on the board (or pick key digits and pattern cells), and `verifyPracticeAnswer` in `src/engine/practiceVerifier.ts` checks my move against `allValidSteps` to distinguish an exact target match, a valid alternative pattern proving a real deduction on the board, a partial match, or a wrong elimination. If I get stuck, `progressiveHints.ts` reveals help in four stages: (1) a house and digit nudge, (2) the technique name and structural mechanism, (3) the participating cells and links without the eliminations, and (4) the full solution.
- **Relaxed local progress tracking:** `practiceStore.ts` and `Progress.tsx` log attempts, recognition rates, and hint usage in `localStorage` with no countdown timers or leaderboards, surfacing **Techniques to Strengthen** (`getStrugglingTechniques`) for patterns where I repeatedly needed hints or multiple tries.
- **Interactive Chain Trainer:** Alternating Inference Chains are hard to hold in your head at first. In `chainTrainer.ts`, clicking candidates directly on the SVG grid draws strong and weak alternating links, reads the causal chain back in plain language as each node is added, flags valid eliminations or continuous loops, and lets me step through the links.

## Live Sync, themes, and verification

When I want to study a tough position mid-solve on Sudoku Exchange without retyping the board, a **Manifest V3 Chrome Extension** (`extension/background.js`, `content-sudokuexchange.js`, and `content-sudokui.js`, wired into Zustand via `syncStore.ts`) mirrors the live board state into oxyDoku, alongside `#import-position=` links, text grid imports, and camera photo scanning. Below is the Live Sync popover connected over one of oxyDoku's nine built-in color palettes, the custom Chess theme.

<OxyDokuCapture capture="sync" />

Because a learning tool teaches the wrong lesson if even one finder makes an unsound elimination, the solver engine and learning subsystems are backed by **1,250+ automated Vitest specs across 82 test files**. `scanner.test.ts` and `soundness.test.ts` verify candidate eliminations across all 76 implemented techniques against a brute-force solution oracle (`tests/solutionOracle.ts`), ensuring no finder ever removes a true solution digit. Separate suites test `stepExplainer.ts`, `progressiveHints.ts`, `practiceVerifier.ts`, `mastery.ts`, and `syncStore.ts`. Beyond the web app, the build pipeline pre-renders **267 localized `/learn/` pages** and **80 SVG technique diagrams**, and packages offline mobile builds through a **Capacitor Android** wrapper.
